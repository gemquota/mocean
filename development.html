Physics atlas · visual reference

How the fish actually moves

These are explanatory models, not decorative swim-cycle illustrations. Each diagram shows a separate part of the physical pathway and where the pathways couple.

01 · Body and world frames

World axes describe habitat travel. Local axes rotate with the fish. Body-forward thrust follows the latter, not the camera.

02 · Force and torque pathways

Forces change linear momentum. Moments change angular momentum. Orientation couples the pathways, but does not replace either.

03 · Heading is not velocity

The nose can point one way while inertia carries the fish another. Reversal requires force over time, not a heading snap.

04 · Orient, then propel

A dive starts by pitching the body. Tail thrust then accelerates along the new body-forward axis, building downward velocity.

05 · Travelling body wave

A continuous curvature wave travels rearward and grows toward the tail. Tail motion drives thrust through interaction with the surrounding water.

06 · Depth and fixed-camera projection

Perspective scale follows focal length divided by camera-space depth. The camera stays fixed; only the fish's projected size and treatment change.
